# Groundwork

## Register

product

## Users

Groundwork is for aspiring founders and people exploring startup ideas. The primary launch audience is people considering micro-SaaS, small software products, and early-stage startups, while the product must also support marketplaces, services, consumer products, and physical products through different analysis playbooks.

Most users begin with an incomplete idea and limited knowledge of what to do next. Their main questions are:

- Should I pursue this idea?
- How should I modify or narrow it?
- What would the journey from idea to first real-world evidence look like?

## Product Purpose

Groundwork helps a user turn a rough startup thought into a clear idea, investigate that idea using current evidence, and understand the broad path to pursuing it.

Its positioning is:

> Stress-test your idea before you spend months building it.

Groundwork reduces uncertainty. It does not claim that AI research alone validates customer demand or proves that a startup will succeed.

The product has three stages:

1. **Shape:** A free AI cofounder conversation that improves and defines the idea.
2. **Investigation:** A paid, live-internet research report that helps the user decide whether and how to pursue the idea.
3. **Roadmap:** A paid, concise overview of the journey ahead, with free contextual follow-up chat.

Success means users receive a direct, useful decision aid instead of a long generic report. The product should make uncertainty, evidence quality, tradeoffs, and next actions understandable.

The commercial objective is to maximize sustainable revenue from paid credits and follow-up analyses while continuing to provide genuine user value. Monetization should be clear, contextual, and should not weaken trust in the research.

## Brand Personality

Quietly confident.

The product voice is direct, constructive, commercially aware, and honest about uncertainty. It challenges ideas without discouraging or flattering the user.

## Anti-references

Groundwork should not resemble:

- A generic SaaS dashboard made from repetitive card grids
- A flashy AI product covered in gradients, glow, glass effects, or decorative animation
- A gamified scoring product that reduces nuanced decisions to arbitrary numbers
- A long PDF reproduced on a webpage
- A form disguised as a chatbot
- A project-management tool with exhaustive tasks, checkboxes, and fake precision
- A research product that presents unsupported estimates as verified facts

## Design Principles

### Decision clarity before information volume

Lead with what the evidence means, why it matters, and what the user should do next.

### Conversation, not interrogation

The Shape assistant contributes ideas, identifies tradeoffs, and asks one high-value question at a time.

### Evidence without theatre

Use live research where promised, distinguish facts from inference, state limitations, and never fabricate precision.

### Structured flexibility

Groundwork owns the interface and core information architecture. AI supplies validated data and may propose relevant optional sections within a controlled component system.

### Monetize meaningful work

Charge credits for research, new ideas, and substantial generation. Do not charge for ordinary clarification of an already purchased Roadmap.

### Calm, focused craftsmanship

Premium quality comes from hierarchy, typography, spacing, useful interactions, and excellent states, not visual noise.

The authenticated product UI does not need to inherit its visual direction from the existing landing page. It may be designed from first principles around the product workflow.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA.

- All core workflows must be keyboard accessible.
- Focus states must be visible.
- Color must never be the only indicator of state.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Interactive controls require clear labels and disabled states.
- Responsive layouts must preserve meaning and usability on smaller screens.
- Charts and visual status displays need accessible text alternatives.
